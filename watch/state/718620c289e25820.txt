Overview
National Science Foundation
funded Simple Ocean Data Assimilation (SODA) project provides retrospectively integrated, spatially complete reconstructions of
the global historical ocean climate. The project goal is to provide a uniform, continuous picture of the evolving global ocean/sea ice system beginning January, 1980.
The algorithm uses sparse physical oceanic/sea ice observations (including satellite SST, sea ice concentration, and in situ temperature and salinity profiles)
as physical constraints to guide the evolution of a high-resolution numerical model. These constraints are applied via a modified version of the
optimal interpolation
data assimilation algorithm (derived based on minimizing a quadratic cost function).
The latest iteration,
SODA4
(specifically the
soda4.15
stream series), represents a step forward from the eddy-permitting architecture of
SODA3. SODA4 introduces a
global eddy-resolving 0.1° horizontal resolution
and enhanced vertical resolution, for more accurate mapping of
near-shore environments, shallow coastal processes,
and narrow currents, and mixed layers where flow instabilities are important. For SODA4 we have also upgraded things such as the topographic map, ocean observations, and discharge data sets.
Dataset Metadata
Data Type
Gridded mesoscale ocean/sea ice reanalysis variables (NetCDF-4 format)
Temporal Range
45-year continuous timeline:
1980 – 2024 currently
Horizontal Resolution
0.1° x 0.1° global grid (~10km at the equator; eddy-resolving)
Vertical Resolution
Enhanced vertical resolution ocean layers
Numeric Numerics
GFDL MOM5 Ocean Model / SIS1 Sea Ice Numerics
Surface Forcing
ECMWF ERA5
Assimilation Method
Optimal Interpolation (OI) sequential analysis algorithm
Observational Constraints
Satellite SST, In situ T/S profiles (WOD/Argo), and Sea Ice Concentration properties
