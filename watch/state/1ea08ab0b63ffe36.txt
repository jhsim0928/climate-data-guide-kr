ABOUT LDOPE
Quality assessment (QA) is an integral part of the MODIS/VIIRS Land production chain. The objective of land products QA is to evaluate and document the scientific quality of the land products with respect to their intended performance. The results of land products QA are made available on a routine basis and are formally stored as product metadata and as per-pixel information. Land products QA results are also placed on the Product Quality web pages located at this site. Users are encouraged to check these QA results when they order and use individual products to ensure that they were generated without error or artifacts.

MOD44B (C61) Vegetation Continuous Fields (VCF) - Retrieval Differences Due to Java Runtime Update
<h1>MOD44B (C61) Vegetation Continuous Fields (VCF) - Retrieval Differences Due to Java Runtime Update</h1><p style="font-size: 16px;"> Users of the MOD44B Collection 6.1 (C61) Vegetation Continuous Fields (VCF) product should be aware of a discontinuity within the C61 record introduced by an update to the Java runtime environment.<br><br>In May 2025, the operational processing environment adopted an updated Java runtime (Java 21) to meet NASA security compliance requirements, replacing the older version (Java 11). Data for <b>2024 onward uses the new Java version</b>. The MOD44B production software (PGE61) is <b>sensitive to Java version variation</b>, meaning identical inputs can yield slightly different retrievals depending on the Java version used.<br><br>As a result, a discontinuity exists within the C61 record. Testing shows these differences are concentrated over <b>mixed vegetation landscapes</b>, where retrievals may show <b>lower percent tree cover</b>, while stable landscapes such as forests show <b>no differences</b> (see Figure 1). These differences reflect a runtime/environment sensitivity and are <b>not</b> a defect in the science algorithm or a change in product quality.</p>

HC_MOD_26008
Note
MODIS - Terra/Aqua Drift
<h1>MODIS - Terra/Aqua Drift</h1>The Terra and Aqua satellites, launched in 1999 and 2002 respectively, have undergone orbit drift adjustments to extend their missions and continue providing critical Earth science measurements. Terra completed its final inclination adjustment maneuver (IAM) and drag make-up (DMU) in 2022, while Aqua completed its IAM and DMU in 2021. Both satellites are expected to contribute to comprehensive Climate Data Records for nearly three decades. However, orbit drift has impacted MODIS data, affecting pixel size, spacing, scan gaps, bowtie overlap, and diurnal cycle sampling. These changes have led to widened scan gaps and altered spatial sampling patterns, with significant impacts observed in late 2024. The effects on MODIS fire products and broader datasets are under <a href="https://landweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/data/filePost/LANCE_UWG_Orbit_Drift_Giglio_202505.pdf">active investigation</a>.<br><br><br>
TM_MOD_25218
Note
MODIS Switch to NISE AMSR2 Input
<h1>MODIS Switch to NISE AMSR2 Input</h1>Effective August 6, 2025 (Day 218), operational MODIS data processing implemented a transition in the PGEs to begin using the NISE AMSR2 (NISE_A2) dataset in place of the legacy NISE input. This change was made in preparation for the planned decommissioning of the original NISE data source. Prior to the switch, NISE_A2 was thoroughly evaluated for data quality and continuity to ensure minimal impact on MODIS Level-2 products. The evaluation, conducted by the LDOPE team, found only minor differences in the MODIS Cloud Mask products (MOD35_L2 and MYD35_L2), primarily in high-latitude regions with persistent snow and ice cover. No downstream effects were observed in products that do not directly ingest the MxD35_L2 cloud mask. <br><br> The comparison graphics below illustrate the classification differences between operational C61 and NISE_A2 data for a representative day in the winter season. The complete evaluation report is available here: <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href='https://landweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/data/filePost/AS2019_NISE_A2_Land.pdf'> LDOPE NISE_A2 Evaluation </a>

TM_MOD_24152
Note
TERRA GPS Shift Anomaly
<h1>TERRA GPS Shift Anomaly </h1>On 2024/132 at 23:00:15z, Terra exceeded its constraints for its on-board navigation system calculations and transitioned from real time orbit determination using TONS to a propagation based state calculation off of the last valid TONS update. The behavior was likely due to the strong solar storm from that weekend, rather than any corrective actions that were undertaken for the shunt anomaly. At 2024/133 at 16:29:55z, the position and velocity covariances returned within tolerance for Terra to transition back to real time calculations of its state (see Figure 1 and Figure 2). No specific corrective actions were required on the part of the Terra flight team, and is likely due to high drag environment induced by the recent solar activity throwing off the models for the TDRS/EOS states onboard the spacecraft.

PM_MOD_23027
Note
Decommissioning of the MODIS C6 Forward Processing
C6 Forward processing of Terra and Aqua MODIS Land data products will be discontinued, on February 15, 2023 (02/15/2023). The C6 land data products have always suffered from known calibration issues that were discovered by the instrument Cal/Val team after the completion of the C6 reprocessing. The main issue among these being the increased impact of optical crosstalk in the Terra PV LWIR bands 27-30, that had made retrieval of most atmospheric parameters infeasible, indirectly impacting some of the land products that were dependent on these atmospheric retrievals. The Collection 6.1 (C61) corrects for this and some other critical calibration issues and also has a number of product specific improvements and enhancements that are listed in the C61 change document<A href="https://landweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/data/userguide/MODIS_Land_C61_Changes.pdf"> here.</A><br><br>Given the improved performance and calibration of C61, users are requested to switch to C61 for all of their MODIS land data needs. DAACs, will retain the C6 historical land data products for a further period of 6 months, following this decommissioning of the C6 forward processing, and will decommission all C6 land data products after the expiry of this 6-month retention period.<br><br>

Terra Constellation Exit
Users of MODIS Science data products are requested to note that MODIS Terra is scheduled to exit the Morning Constellation of Earth Science satellites by October, 2022. Because of onboard fuel shortage, the last of Terra mission maneuver was done on February 27, 2020 to maintain the mean local time (MLT) of 10:30 AM. Thereafter, Terra has been drifting slowly from its MLT and will reach and exceed 10:15 AM MLT by October 2022, when it will make the constellation exit with a lowered orbit altitude of 694kms. More information in this regard can be found <A href="https://modis.gsfc.nasa.gov/news/individual.php?news_id=100378"> here</A> and<A href="https://terra.nasa.gov/about/terra-orbital-drift#:~:text=The%20Constellation%20Exit%20lowers%20the,to%20an%20uncontrolled%20de%2Dorbit."> here.</A><br><br>Please note that this does not indicate the end of life for Terra mission and Terra is expected to remain operational for at least another 5 more years and NASA SIPS will continue to produce and distribute science quality data through Fall of 2025.
