Data Quality
Data quality, which includes quality control, performance monitoring, and calibration and validation, is routinely carried out to ensure that EarthCARE meets its mission and data quality requirements.
If you have any questions about the quality of EarthCARE data please contact the EarthCARE User Support Team by submitting your query or request to
ESA’s User Services Portal.
Quality Control Reports
EarthCARE Quality Control (QC) Reports are produced on a daily, weekly and monthly basis, covering products produced from all three ESA instruments, ATLID, BBR and MSI.
Daily Quality Control Reports
The daily QC reports provide the quality status of the instruments and products from the previous day. Information in the daily QC reports includes a daily production visualization, data completeness, quality flag warnings and failures, and comparison with Numerical Weather Prediction (NWP) forecasts.
Download EarthCARE daily reports
Monthly Quality Control Reports
The monthly QC reports offer a comprehensive overview of the reporting month quality. Information in the monthly QC reports includes instrument status, data coverage, degraded data summary, data quality disclaimers, mission configuration baseline and long-term performance analysis.
Download EarthCARE monthly reports
Quarterly Monitoring Reports
EarthCARE Monitoring Reports are produced on a quarterly basis, assessing the quality of EarthCARE ATLID and CPR data through comparison with the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) global NWP model. Three reports are produced each quarter:
ATLID Aerosols, which assesses ATLID level 2A particulate backscatter coefficient from aerosols
ATLID Cloud, which assesses ATLID level 1B molecular (Rayleigh) attenuated backscatter and total backscatter (Rayleigh plus both Mie channels)
CPR, which assesses CPR level 1B attenuated radar reflectivity

BBR Operations
To extend the instrument lifetime, the Broadband Radiometer (BBR) alternates between Standby mode, during which no observations are taken, and Nominal mode, during which the instrument is active and scientifically valid observations are taken. Over a 20-day period, roughly 15 days (233 orbits) will be in Standby mode and roughly 5 days (78 orbits) will be in Nominal mode.
Further information on the new operating schedule is available in this
EarthCARE BBR life‑extending operations
news item.

After Orbital Control Manoeuvres (OCMs) and Dark Current Calibrations (DCC), the ATL_NOM_1B backscatter exhibits anomalous signatures in all three channels.
This is because of the re-alignment of the LIDAR after orbital maneouvres.

Since 25/03/2025, the affected epochs are flagged and are not further processed to L1, and they appear as gaps in the data.

A-FM Products
In Baseline BD, A-FM products may contain non clear-sky features
which are cut-off around 28km, most commonly occurring in frames containing
high-altitude features associated with strong tropical convection.
This is due to the A-FM processor stratospheric feature detection
configuration being set to a maximum height of 28km.
Users analysing high altitude features around 28km are advised
to take this behaviour into consideration when using this product.

GPS Outage
EarthCARE data may occasionally be affected by degraded Global Positioning System (GPS) Position, Velocity, and Time (PVT) measurements. Caution is advised when these data are used in applications requiring high precision geopositioning, such as Doppler velocity assessments. The complete list of frames affected by GPS flags since the data release is provided below.

Reprocessing Quality Updates
EarthCARE data will be reprocessed periodically. The first full reprocessing of EarthCARE L1 and L2 1- and 2-sensor products occurred during the summer of 2025, reprocessing the data to Baseline BA. Relevant quality information related to reprocessed data will be provided here.

Orbit Prediction and Overpass Tool

EarthCARE overpasses of radioastronomy (RAS) sites are also available from the Payload Data Ground Segment (PDGS).

Calibration/Validation
Calibration activities ensure that the EarthCARE instruments are performing well and to their established baselines. Validation activities ensure that the EarthCARE data and products are meaningful and well characterised with respect to uncertainties due to sensitivity or resolution.
